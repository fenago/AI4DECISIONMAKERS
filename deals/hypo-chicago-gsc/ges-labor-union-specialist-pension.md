## 4. Does a GES purchase trigger withdrawal? (counsel to confirm)
- **Stock purchase:** the contributing entity survives, so **no withdrawal at closing**. The contingent WL moves to GES's controlled group, and GES's whole group becomes jointly and severally liable if a withdrawal happens later.
- **Asset purchase:** this **triggers the seller's withdrawal** unless the deal meets **ERISA §4204**. Under §4204, the buyer assumes contributions for substantially the same base units and posts a bond or escrow for 5 plan years. The seller stays secondarily liable for 5 years and must post its own bond if it distributes substantially all its assets. The buyer takes on the seller's contribution history.
- **Consolidating into GES crews:** if GES already contributes to the same plans (*Assumption*), moving the work to a GES entity that pays into the same plan for the same work is generally not a withdrawal, because the controlled group is treated as one employer. Moving the work to a GES entity under a **different** plan or CBA could trigger one.
- **Partial-withdrawal trap:** a **70% decline in contributions** over the 3-year test, or partial cessation, triggers a partial withdrawal. Losing either of the two organizers that make up 40% of revenue could set this off.
- **Special industry rules:** the construction-industry rule (§4203(b)) and the entertainment-industry rule (§4203(c)) *might* apply to trade-show carpentry and could reduce WL sharply. **This is a counsel question.** Do not assume either rule applies.

## 5. CBAs, jurisdiction, cost pass-through
- **CBA expiry dates:** unknown. Request them. A Teamsters or Carpenters expiry within 12 months of closing is a pricing issue.
- **Jurisdiction:** at McCormick, carpenter work is a unified Carpenter/Decorator force, so overlap with GES carpenters is low risk. Combining **Teamsters freight and warehouse** work is the main grievance risk, including whether the target's warehouse is under the same CBA as GES's.
- **Pass-through:** GSC labor is billed to exhibitors from published rate cards, so pass-through is generally good. The lag is on multi-year organizer contracts with fixed labor rates and on fixed-price design-build bids. *Assumption.*

## 6. Due-diligence requests
1. List of every plan with 5–10 years of contribution history, plus the §101(l) WL estimate from each plan.
2. Annual Funding Notices, zone certifications, Form 5500 Schedule MB, and any rehabilitation or funding-improvement plan.
3. All CBAs (venue, shop, warehouse) with expiry, successorship, and subcontracting clauses.
4. Payroll audits and delinquency notices from the plans, grievances, NLRB charges, and strike or work-stoppage history.
5. Workers classified as 1099, especially design and installation freelancers.
6. A labor cost bridge: union hours, straight-time vs. overtime, and labor as a % of revenue by line of business.

## 7. Deal protection
Use a stock deal, or an asset deal that strictly complies with §4204. Add a specific pension indemnity outside the general cap, with escrow sized to the plan estimates. Add an R&W on contributions and the absence of any prior partial withdrawal. Make the plan estimates a closing condition. Have GES and the seller meet Local 727 and the Carpenters together before announcement.
