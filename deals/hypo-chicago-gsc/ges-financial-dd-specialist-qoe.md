# Project Lakeshore — Quality of Earnings Framework (Round 1)

**Author:** ges-financial-dd-specialist · **Date:** 2026-11-17 · **Status:** HYPOTHETICAL case, no data room.
**Bottom line:** Base-case diligence-adjusted EBITDA is **~$6.0M (Assumption)**, plausible range **$5.0M–$7.2M**, against reported ~$8.0M (given). Maintenance capex on rental inventory likely takes cash EBITDA (EBITDA − capex) to **~$4.0–4.5M**. At the asking ~$72M, the buyer would pay **~12x adjusted EBITDA**, not 9x.

Every target figure below is an **Assumption** unless marked otherwise. No numbers come from the target, because none exist. This is the framework plus estimates sized from industry knowledge. The document requests at the end are what would turn each Assumption into a Verified figure.

## 1. Sanity check: is 13.3% plausible?
- **Reported (Viad Q4/FY2023 release, Business Wire, 8 Feb 2024):** GES revenue $888.4M with GES Adjusted EBITDA $68.2M in FY2023 (~7.7%), and $828.0M with $61.3M in FY2022 (~7.4%). https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240208093688/en/Viad-Corp-Reports-2023-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-Results
- **Judgment:** A $60M regional GSC plus design-build shop reporting 13.3% is running at about 1.7x the margin of a scaled national contractor. Design-build mix can support a higher margin. Even so, an outlier margin at a family-owned business usually means the costs that a buyer must carry are understated: market rent, market management pay, maintenance capex, current union rates. Assume the gap is partly accounting until proven otherwise.

## 3. Capex and cash conversion
- Maintenance capex covers rental inventory (carpet, furniture, booth systems, graphics hardware), fabrication equipment, trucks and forklifts. **Assumption: 2.5–3.5% of revenue, $1.5–2.1M a year.** Build it from a fixed-asset roll-forward plus a physical count and aging of the rental inventory.
- Base EBITDA − maintenance capex ≈ **$4.0–4.5M**. ges-valuation-analyst should price that cash figure, not EBITDA alone.
- Watch for deferred capex: a family seller often stops refreshing the rental inventory in the two to three years before a sale.

## 4. Net working capital peg
- Cash cycle: organizers and exhibitors pay deposits weeks to months before a show, and labor, freight and material costs come after. **NWC is likely negative at seasonal peaks** (Assumption).
- **Recommendation:** set the peg on a **24-month average of monthly NWC** (the odd/even-year cycle needs 24 months, not 12), excluding cash and debt-like items.
- **Watch item:** the seller can time the close just after big deposit collections and keep the cash. Customer deposits for shows that GES will service after closing must either stay in the business or count as debt.

## 5. Debt-like items (to deduct from enterprise value)
1. **Multiemployer pension withdrawal liability:** sizing belongs to **ges-labor-union-specialist**. Listed here and not quantified by me.
2. Deferred revenue and customer deposits whose costs are still to be incurred. At minimum, deduct the cost-to-serve portion (Assumption: 70–85% of the balance).
3. Accrued but unpaid bonuses, PTO, and union vacation and holiday fund obligations.
4. Unpaid income taxes. That includes Illinois replacement tax at the entity level for a pass-through, and any sales/use tax exposure on fabrication and rentals.
5. ERC clawback and audit exposure, if ERC was claimed. Get an indemnity or escrow.
6. Equipment loans, vehicle and finance leases, and the related-party lease if it is above market.
7. Customer credits or refund obligations carried over from cancelled shows.
8. Self-insured workers' comp and health reserves, if any.
9. Deferred compensation, phantom equity or retention promises made to family or key staff.
10. Deferred maintenance capex on rental inventory, treated as a price adjustment.

## 6. Red flags, ranked
1. A 13.3% margin against GES at ~7.7% (Reported, above). The burden of proof sits with the seller.
2. The LTM may include a biennial or rotating show peak.
3. Related-party real estate, which affects both the rent restatement and what is actually in the perimeter.
4. 40% of revenue from two organizers (given). Contract renewal dates drive how much of the revenue repeats. ges-show-organizer-expert has this.
5. The size of the pension liability could change the price more than any EBITDA adjustment.
