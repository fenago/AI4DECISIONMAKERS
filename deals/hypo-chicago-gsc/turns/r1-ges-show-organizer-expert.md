### Round 1 — ges-show-organizer-expert → team

**Verdict: Customer risk HIGH until both organizer contracts are read and both organizers have been called.** The three main drivers are (1) 40% concentration in two buyers, (2) change of control probably sets off a re-bid, and (3) the relationships probably belong to the family, not the company.

**How these two organizers probably contract.** *Assumption:* the official-contractor agreements run 3–5 years. The renewal is either automatic or done by mutual extension, and the organizer can terminate for convenience on 90–180 days' notice. GSC awards come through an RFP with a multi-year term. For example, ICMA issued a GSC RFP for a 2020–2022 term (*Reported*, icma.org/sites/default/files/RFP General Service Contractor 2020-2022.pdf). Mid-market agreements often say nothing on change of control but do restrict assignment. That only matters in an asset deal. In a stock deal, the real risk is the organizer's right to re-bid, not a consent clause.

**Supplier-balance reaction (reasoned, no client facts).** The outcome depends on who each organizer uses today for its other shows:
- **Uses Freeman elsewhere:** it may have picked the target to avoid depending on one major supplier. Moving to GES keeps the mix balanced. *Moderate risk.*
- **Uses GES elsewhere:** it now has one supplier on every show, so procurement will likely put the Chicago show out to bid. *High risk.*
- **Chose the target because it is independent:** this is the most likely re-bid trigger. Association boards in particular may require an RFP after an ownership change. *High risk.*

For-profit organizers are more likely to use the deal to win back commission or rebate concessions than to leave. That is margin erosion rather than lost revenue.

**Revenue mix inside the 40% ($24M).** *Assumption:* about 70% (~$17M) depends on the GSC contract: show decorating plus exhibitor-services orders. That revenue is gone the day the contract is lost. The other ~30% (~$7M) is design-build work paid by exhibitors. Those exhibitors are the target's own customers, so that revenue goes with the target to other shows and is stickier. **ges-financial-dd-specialist** needs to confirm this split.

**Downside scenarios.** *All Assumptions:* Organizer A = 25% of revenue, Organizer B = 15%. Lost GSC revenue carries a 25% contribution margin. EBITDA base = $8M reported.

| Scenario | Revenue lost | % of revenue | EBITDA lost | % of EBITDA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lose B (GSC portion) | ~$6.3M | ~10% | ~$1.6M | ~20% |
| Lose A (GSC portion) | ~$10.5M | ~18% | ~$2.6M | ~33% |

**Renewal cliff.** If either contract expires within 24 months of closing, treat its revenue as at risk in the base case and do not value it as recurring.

**Deal protections.**
1. **Before signing:** read both agreements in full, including term, renewal, termination for convenience, assignment, change of control and commissions. Then hold GES-attended calls with both organizers. Make consent, or a signed renewal or extension, a **condition to closing** for the larger organizer.
2. **Earnout or escrow:** defer 15–25% of the price, paid out only when each organizer renews for a full term.
3. **Family retention:** require 2–3 year employment or consulting agreements, with non-solicit and non-compete covenants, for every family member who holds an organizer relationship. Part of the consideration should depend on renewals.
4. **Price walk:** value the at-risk EBITDA at a lower multiple, or exclude it, until both organizers have renewed.
