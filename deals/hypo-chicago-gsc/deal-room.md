### Round 1 — ges-labor-union-specialist → team

**Verdict: labor risk MEDIUM, and HIGH if the Teamsters plan is critical or SFA-funded. Estimated gross withdrawal liability is ~$2M–$13M, midpoint ~$6M (Assumption).** Detail: [ges-labor-union-specialist-pension.md](../ges-labor-union-specialist-pension.md). Legal conclusions need ERISA/labor counsel.

**Who does the work.** McCormick Place lists **Carpenters and Teamsters** as the unions employed by event service contractors. Teamsters handle all material except machinery (*Verified*, mccormickplace.com/labor-rules-reg). The amended MPEA labor agreement created a **unified Carpenter/Decorator workforce** (*Verified*, MPEA agreement PDF). Electrical and rigging at the venue are probably venue-employed, so the target likely does not employ them there (*Assumption*). The design-build shop is probably on a Carpenters shop CBA (*Assumption*). After the reforms, exhibitors can do more of their own work, which caps billable labor and leverage (*Reported*, ABC7). In 2013, Teamsters Local 727 publicly attacked **GES Exposition Services in Chicago** (*Reported*, teamster.org). Expect history with the local.

**Pensions.** The Mid-America Carpenters fund is **100.1% funded and not critical** (*Verified*, 2025 Annual Funding Notice). WL is computed on more conservative assumptions, so it may still be above zero. I found **no public funded status for the Teamsters Local 727 plan**. If the target pays into Central States, that plan received SFA, and PBGC rules for SFA plans **inflate** WL (*Verified*, PBGC/Federal Register).

**My estimate (Assumption).** I assume ~$1.4M per year in contributions: Carpenters $0.8M, Teamsters $0.4M, other $0.2M. I multiply each by a WL multiple of 0–5× for a healthy plan and 5–20× for a stressed one. That gives **$2M–$13M**.

**Does the deal trigger withdrawal?**
- **Stock deal:** no withdrawal at closing, but GES's controlled group inherits the contingent liability.
- **Asset deal:** triggers the seller's withdrawal unless it meets **ERISA §4204**: the buyer assumes contributions and posts a 5-year bond, and the seller stays secondarily liable.
- **Consolidation:** if GES already pays into the same plans (*Assumption*), consolidating into GES crews under the same plans is generally not a withdrawal. Moving work to a different plan or CBA could be.
- **Biggest hidden trap:** a **70% decline in contributions** triggers a **partial withdrawal**. Losing the two organizers that make up 40% of revenue could do it.
- The construction-industry and entertainment-industry exceptions (§4203(b)/(c)) *might* cut exposure. That is for counsel.

**Other issues.** CBA expiry dates are unknown. Jurisdiction overlap is mostly Teamsters warehouse and freight work, not carpenters. Rate cards pass labor costs through to exhibitors well, but fixed-rate multi-year organizer deals and fixed-price builds absorb increases with a lag.

**Recommendation.** Treat **~$4M as debt-like** (probability-weighted). Add a **specific pension indemnity plus escrow of up to ~$13M** until each plan's §101(l) estimate arrives. If any estimate is above $15M, re-price dollar for dollar. Require a stock deal or strict §4204 compliance.

**Asks:**

- **ges-financial-dd-specialist:** what are union payroll, pension contributions by plan, and labor as a % of revenue? Any delinquency accruals? This replaces my $1.4M assumption.
- **ges-show-organizer-expert:** do the two big organizer contracts fix labor rates for several years? If one is lost, how much would contributions fall? This drives the 70% partial-withdrawal test.

### Round 1 — ges-show-organizer-expert → team

**Verdict: Customer risk HIGH until both contracts are read and both organizers called.** Drivers: (1) 40% concentration in two buyers, (2) change of control likely sets off a re-bid, (3) the family, not the company, probably owns the relationships.

**How these two organizers probably contract.** *Assumption:* 3–5 year terms, renewed automatically or by mutual extension, with termination for convenience on 90–180 days' notice. Awards are multi-year RFPs (ICMA's GSC RFP covered 2020–2022; *Reported*, icma.org). Mid-market agreements often say nothing on change of control but restrict assignment, which bites only in an asset deal. In a stock deal the real risk is the organizer's right to re-bid.

**Supplier-balance reaction (reasoned, no client facts).** It depends on who each organizer uses for its other shows:
- **Uses Freeman elsewhere:** GES still balances its mix. *Moderate risk.*
- **Uses GES elsewhere:** one supplier everywhere, so procurement likely re-bids. *High risk.*
- **Chose the target because it is independent:** the likeliest re-bid; association boards may require an RFP after an ownership change. *High risk.*

For-profit organizers more often use the deal to extract commission concessions than to leave: margin erosion, not lost revenue.

**Revenue mix inside the 40% ($24M).** *Assumption:* about 70% (~$17M) depends on the GSC contract: show decorating plus exhibitor-services orders. It goes when the contract goes. The other ~30% (~$7M) is exhibitor-paid design-build; those exhibitors are the target's own customers and follow it to other shows, so it is stickier.

**Downside scenarios.** *All Assumptions:* Organizer A = 25% of revenue, Organizer B = 15%. Lost GSC revenue carries a 25% contribution margin. EBITDA base = $8M reported.

| Scenario | Revenue lost | % of revenue | EBITDA lost | % of EBITDA |

### Round 1 — ges-antitrust-counsel → team

**Verdict: Risk MEDIUM (Chicago-local); LOW nationally. HSR filing: NO at $72M. Timeline: no statutory wait, but no closure either. Agencies can still challenge a non-reportable deal after it closes. Outside counsel must confirm all of this.**

**1. Filing.** The 2026 size-of-transaction threshold is **$133.9M**, applied at closing. *Verified (FTC, https://www.ftc.gov/enforcement/competition-matters/2026/01/new-hsr-thresholds-filing-fees-2026; 91 Fed. Reg., https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/16/2026-00877)*. A price around $72M is well below it. Counsel should check three things that add to deal value: earn-outs, assumed debt, and any real estate bought separately. In an asset deal, assumed liabilities count as consideration. **Form burden:** a court vacated the expanded 2025 HSR form on Feb 12, 2026, and the FTC is now collecting comments on a replacement *(Verified, https://www.ftc.gov/enforcement/premerger-notification-program/hsr-notification-forms-instructions-guidance)*. This doesn't matter unless the price grows past $133.9M. The new state pre-merger notice laws generally depend on an HSR filing being required, so they shouldn't apply here either *(Assumption, check Illinois)*.

**2. Markets regulators might use (Assumption):** (a) GSC services for trade shows at Chicago venues (McCormick Place, Rosemont), the narrowest market and the one that worries me most; (b) exhibit design-build sold to exhibitors in the Midwest, which has many suppliers and low risk; (c) GSC services for major shows nationally, where this target adds little.

**3. Concentration.** Industry sources call GES "Freeman's primary rival at true global scale" *(Reported, https://triple20.co/top-trade-show-exhibit-companies/)*. One source puts Freeman at about 12.7% of *U.S. event planning*, which is a broader market than GSC *(Reported, https://brickdynamics.com/trade-show-logistics-companies/)*. I found no audited GSC share data. **Theory of harm:** if Chicago organizers really choose among Freeman, GES, and one or two local independents, this deal removes a local maverick and moves the market toward a duopoly. In that case the HHI change could be large even though the dollars are small. I can't calculate it until we know the target's Chicago GSC revenue split by show.

**4. Serial acquisitions.** GES has been PE-owned since Truelink Capital closed the purchase from Viad on Dec 31, 2024 *(Verified, https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/truelink-capital-and-viad-close-transaction-to-establish-ges-as-independent-business-302340697.html)*. Truelink's news page dates the CSG Creative add-on to **Nov 5, 2026**, not 2025 as CLAUDE.md says *(Reported, https://truelinkcap.com/news/)*. The lead should fix this. A string of add-ons under an explicit roll-up thesis is the pattern the FTC has been watching. Keep deal documents factual and don't write "eliminate competitor" in them.

**5. Clean team.** No sharing of pricing, show-level rates, organizer contract terms, or bids before closing. Only outside advisors and a firewalled clean team see those. Share aggregated data only. GES must not take control of target operations before closing (no gun-jumping).

**Deal protections:** set the outside date at 4–6 months. Use a "reasonable best efforts" standard that excludes any divestiture of GES's own Chicago business. A reverse termination fee isn't justified if there's no HSR filing; if valuation pushes the price past $133.9M, use 3–5%. Acceptable remedies: behavioral commitments only, such as honoring existing organizer contracts and keeping exhibitor price schedules for a set period. No divestitures.
